Even out your bass shakers

Bass shakers rarely move the seat evenly. Some frequencies hit hard and others barely register, and you can't tell by listening, because you feel it. This wizard measures what your seat really does, then builds a small EQ that evens it out.

15 to 20 minutesFree Phyphox appRuns in your browserminiDSP or any EQ

How it works

  1. A slow sweep plays from low to high bass through your normal system.
  2. Your phone sits on the seat and records the vibration with its accelerometer, using the free Phyphox app.
  3. You upload the recording and the wizard works out a handful of parametric EQ filters.
  4. You enter the filters in your DSP or EQ, or export a miniDSP preset. Then measure again to check, and refine if you like.

What you need

  • About 15 to 20 minutes the first time. The sweeps take under 3 minutes. Repeat runs are quicker.
  • A device that plays audio into your shakers. Open this page on it. The sweep plays from the page, at whatever volume you set.
  • A phone with Phyphox (free on Android and iOS), plus a way to move its exported file to the device you are using, such as email, a cloud drive, USB or a nearby share.
  • A way to apply EQ to the shaker channel: a DSP such as a miniDSP, an AVR or amplifier with parametric EQ, or an app. The wizard gives you the filters as a list, and as a miniDSP preset file if you use one.
  • A still phone. It needs to stay put on the seat for about 3 minutes while the sweeps play.
Everything runs in your browser. Your recordings and settings are not uploaded anywhere. Before any sound plays you get two warnings and a countdown, and a Stop button is always on screen.

Not ready to measure? The demo needs no sound and no phone. It simulates a shaker so you can see what the wizard produces.

Already have a sweep recording from before?

If you recorded this wizard's sweeps earlier, you can skip the setup and go straight to uploading the recording.

The method comes from Tyler Gauharou's Bass Shaker EQ workbook, described on Simple Home Cinema in Revolutionizing Bass Shaker Equalization. This wizard is an independent version of the same idea. It adds automatic sweeps, filter fitting and file export, and it does not contain or reuse his workbook.

Set the measurement level

A steady tone plays while you raise your level control until Phyphox reads 2. This fixes the level for everything that follows, so it is the one setting you must not touch afterwards.

Starting point: shaker amplifier gain at the value you chose in the first step (maximum is recommended), your level control (AVR master volume or trim, or DSP gain) turned all the way down, and this computer at 100%. Raise only the level control.

Reference tone: 35 Hz, the middle of your rated range. It is chosen automatically.

Phyphox setup
  1. Open Phyphox and choose Acceleration with g. This is the raw accelerometer, which is what we want.
  2. Lay the phone flat on the seat, screen up, where you sit. Mark the spot with tape. Use the same spot every time.
  3. Press play in Phyphox. The gauge rescales as the reading grows, in steps of 2 (0 to 2, then 0 to 4 and so on), so the first scale ends at 2. The big number flickers with a steady tone, so judge by its peaks.
Raise your level control slowly, from minimum, until the peaks in Phyphox reach 2, then stop. Do not go past 2. If you cannot reach 2 comfortably, stop at a level you are happy with; a lower level only adds a little noise to the result.

Record the sweeps

Three slow sweeps at three levels, 6 dB apart. Recording all three shows whether your setup is linear, and gives each frequency the best signal to noise.

  1. Check the phone is in its marked spot and nothing else is touching the seat.
  2. Start recording in Phyphox (the play button), then do not touch the phone.
  3. Wait at least 5 seconds, then press Play sweeps below. Stay quiet and still.
  4. When the sequence ends, wait a few seconds, then stop the recording.
  5. In Phyphox open the menu, choose Export data, pick CSV or Excel, and get the file onto this computer. Menu names vary a little between versions.

Something not working?
  • It can't find the sweeps, or says the recording is too short. Record the whole sequence: start Phyphox at least 5 seconds before you press Play, and stop a few seconds after the sound ends.
  • The sample rate is too low. Use Acceleration with g in Phyphox. The "without g" experiment is capped at about 200 samples per second.
  • The three levels don't scale evenly. A compressor or limiter is still on, or an amplifier is clipping. Turn them off, or lower the level a little and repeat.
  • Signal is only a little above the noise. Raise the level slightly, or tape the phone flat to a firm part of the seat. A soft cushion soaks up vibration.
  • The file won't open. Export from Phyphox as Excel, CSV or zip, and don't edit it first.

Calculate the filters

The filters are worked out automatically when you open this page. The fit chooses a small set of parametric filters that bring the measured response toward your target, within your boost and cut limits.

Check and refine

Load the filters, run the same sweeps again, and see how close the result is. A second pass usually tightens the match.

  1. Enter the filters in your DSP and set the output gain shown in the previous step. Leave every other setting, and the amplifier gain, exactly as they were.
  2. Repeat the sweep recording with the phone in the same spot.
  3. Upload the new file below.

Warning 1 of 2: loud sound and vibration

The next step plays low-frequency sound and drives your shakers. It can be loud, shake the seat and floor, and carry through the building.

  • Nobody should be sitting on the seat unprepared. Check pets and children.
  • You can stop at any time with the Stop button, or by muting the amplifier.
